Abstract

The accuracy of search results is increasingly purchased at inference time rather than baked into a single model. Measured by result quality, most state-of-the-art systems retrieve a candidate pool, rerank it with a cross-encoder, and increasingly re-order the remaining candidates with a listwise reranker. Each addition spends more compute per query, and each raises the same question of where the next unit of compute is best spent: depth, stages, or width? We map all three levers on five subsets of BRIGHT, a benchmark designed to test reasoning-intensive information retrieval, using hybrid retrieval that combines Weaviate BM25 with Snowflake Arctic 2.0 embeddings, three commercial cross-encoders: Cohere rerank-v4.0-pro, Voyage rerank-2.5, and ZeroEntropy zerank-2, and three listwise LLM rerankers from OpenAI: GPT-5.4 Mini, GPT-5.6 Luna, and GPT-5.6 Terra.

Depth and stages compose into a quality ladder. Deepening retrieval from k=100 to k=2,000 nearly doubles the share of relevant documents available to later stages from 0.476 to 0.871, and each added stage converts more of that availability into a more accurate ranking. Mean nDCG@10 climbs from 0.181 under hybrid retrieval alone, to 0.424 when the strongest cross-encoder reranks the deepest pool, and 0.470 when a listwise pass re-orders the retained top-20 from the cross-encoder. Increasing depth further at the listwise stage extends the ladder less than expected. Retaining 100 rather than 20 candidates from the cross-encoder places substantially more relevant documents within the listwise stage’s reach, yet the listwise pass only gains 0.012 nDCG@10 at five times the inference cost.

The case for width comes from the models themselves. At each reranking stage, the three models uniquely solve roughly fifty queries their peers miss. However, equal-weight fusion converts almost none of this disagreement, at best marginally exceeding the best single cross-encoder and falling short of the strongest listwise model. Depth and stages are therefore the reliable levers of today’s retrieve-then-rerank systems, while width under static fusion is not, marking query-dependent routing as its promising form for future work. Our experimental code is available on GitHub at github.com/weaviate/scaling-reranked-retrieval.
